\section{QmisID: Charge Misidentification}

Across all channels, the di-Higgs search is challenging the limits of the LHC.

Around \(5000\) di-Higgs events are expected in the full \(147\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}\) Run-2 dataset, three orders of magnitude fewer than single-Higgs events \cite{atlas2024dihiggsbriefing}. To maximize sensitivity to this rare process, the ATLAS Collaboration performs a combined search across several decay channels. The largest branching fraction is\(HH \rightarrow b\bar{b}b\bar{b},\) at \(34\%\). However, large multijet backgrounds make this channel experimentally challenging. An alternative strategy is the \textbf{multi-lepton channel}, sometimes referred to as ``di-Higgs soup'', which combines the decay modes
\(
HH \rightarrow W^+W^-,\ ZZ^*,\ \tau_{had}\tau_{had},
\)
leading to final states containing leptons, jets, and missing transverse momentum. Although the total branching fraction is only about \(12\%\), the reduced Standard Model backgrounds provide competitive sensitivity to di-Higgs production. 


The focus of this paper is the \textbf{two-lepton same-charge (2LSC) channel}, one of the nine sub-channels in the multi-lepton decay. It consists of two light leptons (electrons or muons) of the same charge, and sensitivity is currently limited by QmisID background estimation uncertainties.

QmisID events occur when when a reco label of same-charge (SC) is mistakenly assigned to a two-lepton event which was opposite-charge (OC) in truth. 
Two physical mechanisms can lead to charge misidentification. First, the curvature of a charged particle's trajectory in a uniform magnetic field asymptotically approaches zero at high transverse momentum. Minor deflection of the track, for instance due to scattering inside the detector, can lead to a charge flip. 

The second mechanism is bremsstrahlung followed by photon conversion:
an electron interacts with the detector material, radiates a photon,
and that photon may itself convert into an \(e^{+}e^{-}\) pair.
Above \(\sim\!10\)~MeV, bremsstrahlung is the dominant energy-loss
mechanism \cite{wichmann2008diplomarbeit}. Charge misassignment then occurs when one conversion
electron carries nearly all of the original photon energy and the
reconstruction algorithm follows that secondary track instead of the
original one; the probability of this grows with photon
energy~\cite{wichmann2008diplomarbeit}. The effect has a strong functional dependence on pseudorapidity \(\eta\), as the density of the inner detector increases from 0 to 2.5 in absolute value of \(\eta\). Pseudorapidity \(\eta\) is a spatial coordinate, where \(\theta\) is the polar angle between the particle's momentum vector and the beam axis.


\begin{equation}
    \eta = -\ln\!\left(\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
